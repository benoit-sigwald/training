Delegating
with Claude Cowork.

In chat, you bring your work to Claude. With Cowork, you bring Claude to your work: you describe an outcome, you step away, you come back to a deliverable.

Where the line falls

The rule fits in one sentence, and this is where almost everyone gets stuck at first.

The rule

A question, or a deliverable?

Stay in chat if what you want fits in a few exchanges: a question, an explanation, a brainstorm, a gut check.

Move to Cowork if what you need is a file — a document someone will open, a deck someone will present, a spreadsheet to be sorted. And for anything multi-step, anything touching more than one file or more than one app, or anything you would describe as a task rather than a question.[1]

The most common mistake: reaching for chat for everything, and never feeling the difference. The opposite mistake: handing Cowork a one-off question chat would have answered in five seconds.
ChatWhat should I cover in our business review meeting?
CoworkRead the last three months of meeting notes in this Google Drive folder and build me a QBR deck using our template.
ChatHow do I VLOOKUP something?
CoworkGo through my spreadsheets and change all the VLOOKUP to INDEX MATCH.
ChatSuggest a better title tag and meta description for this page.
CoworkUse the new title tags and meta descriptions for these 30 pages from this sheet and update them using the CMS connector.

The five ingredients of a Cowork-shaped task

You do not need all five. A good candidate hits a few.

  1. More than one thing goes in

    Multiple files, a whole folder, or a file plus some connectors. If there is only one input, chat probably handles it fine.

  2. A file comes out

    A deliverable you can attach, present, share or repurpose: a doc, a deck, a spreadsheet, a CSV.

  3. You'll do it again

    One-offs are fine, but recurring tasks are the sweet spot. They can be scheduled to run before you are even at your desk.

  4. You already know what good looks like

    You are familiar with the shape of the output, so you can tell in fifteen seconds whether it is right, wrong, or 70% there.

  5. The middle is the boring part

    The thinking lives at the start — deciding what you want — and at the end — deciding if it is right. Everything in between, extract, compile, reconcile and reformat, is what you hand off.[1]

Your first ten minutes

Four moves, in this order.

01

Give it something to work with

Drop in files, point it at a folder on your computer, or connect an app you already use. The difference between a mediocre output and a great one is almost never your prompt, but whether you provided enough rich context to work with.[1]

02

Tell it the outcome you want

Not the steps: the deliverable. What you want to be holding at the end, and the context needed to get there.

03

Start with a real task you know well

You will see immediately where it is strong, where it needs context from you — and you already know what "good" means for that particular task.

04

Make it ask you questions before it starts

This is the single most useful habit of the lot. Add this line to your request:

Before we begin, repeat my ask back to me so we're aligned, then ask me as many clarifying questions as you have.

This surfaces what you did not think to specify: which time period, what "good" means here, which edge cases you know and it does not. Answering five questions up front costs thirty seconds. Finding those same gaps afterwards costs time, tokens, and is a pain to fix.[1]

The trap: assuming Claude already knows what is obvious to you.
Not sure what to hand off? Ask it. Claude has memory and can search your past conversations: it can tell you which tasks you do most often, and which ones are worth trying in Cowork.[1]

Three workspaces, one engine

Chat, Cowork and Claude Code are different workspaces. The same models run inside all three.

The split

It is not a question of old versus new

Chat is for when the output is a thought in your head: talking through a positioning problem, pressure-testing an idea before committing to it, asking a random question.

Cowork is for when the output is something you will hand to someone else.

Claude Code is made for developers building and shipping software. Many people do not know that Cowork and Claude Code run on the same engine under the hood.[1]
